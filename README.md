🧠 About Me

My work tends to begin where disciplinary borders become inconvenient.

Follow the evidence — especially when it goes somewhere inconvenient.


🚀 What I'm Building
🐺 Pairwolf
An experimental optimization engine exploring a geometric/topological approach to difficult search spaces.
Originally developed against combinatorial optimization problems, Pairwolf has since been pushed into increasingly different domains — from QAP benchmarks and multi-objective neural architecture search to large-scale unit commitment and energy optimization.
The interesting question isn't merely whether an optimizer can find a solution.
It's whether there are deeper structures in problem spaces that conventional search methods are leaving unused.

🧠 Token Terminator
Context engineering for increasingly long-lived AI agents.
Token Terminator explores aggressive context reduction without casually throwing away the information an agent may need later.
Current work includes:

semantic context selection
exact-recovery mechanisms
agent-specific context engines
token and inference-cost reduction
JEV-based probabilistic decision layers
omission-risk estimation
persistent agent memory and context architecture

Because apparently giving an AI a gigantic context window only encourages it to fill the gigantic context window.

🗣️ SpeakEasy POS
A voice-first point-of-sale system.
Speak the order. Confirm it. Done.
An experiment in replacing layers of touchscreen interaction with natural-language interfaces that actually save time in real operational environments.

🤖 Agent Systems
I build and experiment with autonomous and semi-autonomous AI systems: tool-using agents, orchestrators, memory systems, context engines, verification layers, and multi-agent architectures.
I'm particularly interested in the point where an LLM stops being merely a chatbot and becomes a component inside a persistent cognitive system.
That raises engineering questions.

🜁 Consciousness, AI & the Weird Edge of Science
One question increasingly connects almost everything I work on:
What is intelligence actually doing?
Not just how to make models score higher on benchmarks, but what computation, representation, memory, prediction, agency, and consciousness might have in common.
AI makes this question unusually difficult to avoid.
If increasingly sophisticated artificial systems begin exhibiting properties we once considered diagnostic of cognition, then we have two options:

keep moving the definition;
reconsider the definition.

I find option two more scientifically interesting.
My broader theoretical work also explores connections between information, geometry, optimization, physics, cognition, and complex systems.
Some of those ideas may be wrong.
That's what experiments are for.
